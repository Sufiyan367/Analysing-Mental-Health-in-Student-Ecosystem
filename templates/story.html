Scene 1 — Student Mental Health Baseline

Core Question: What is the overall distress prevalence across the student cohort?

  • 78.0% of students (156 of 200) experience moderate-to-high stress levels (53.5% Medium, 24.5% High).
  • The cohort exhibits an elevated symptom baseline with an average anxiety score of 52.59 and depression score of 48.09.
  • Students average 7.10 hours/day of continuous screen exposure across study and device habits.
Transition: Having established the population baseline, how do these self-reported stress tiers translate into quantifiable anxiety and depression symptom loads?

Scene 2 — Stress and Psychological Symptoms

Core Question: How do anxiety and depression scores escalate across stress tiers?

  • High Stress students average 72.06 in anxiety and 68.78 in depression, compared to 30.27 and 26.80 in Low Stress (a >135% symptom surge).
  • Anxiety and depression increase concurrently across stress categories, confirming joint psychological burden.
  • High-stress individuals operate near clinical distress thresholds on standardized scales.
Transition: Given the severity of psychological symptoms in high-stress students, what lifestyle habits (sleep patterns, screentime immersion) distinguish this cohort?

Scene 3 — Lifestyle Factors and Stress

Core Question: What behavioral vectors (sleep, screen time, physical exercise) correlate with high stress?

  • 71.4% (35 of 49) of High Stress students report Poor sleep, while only 2 maintain Good sleep.
  • High Stress students log 8.12 hours/day of screen exposure (+2.12 hrs more than Low Stress students at 6.00 hrs/day).
  • 75.5% (37 of 49) of High Stress students engage in Low physical activity, showing a clear triad of sleep deprivation, digital immersion, and physical inactivity.
Transition: Beyond daily lifestyle habits, how do pre-existing mental health history, support networks, and psychiatric medication intersect with student vulnerability?

Scene 4 — Vulnerability Factors and Support Systems

Core Question: How do prior mental-health history and social support influence student resilience?

  • 40.0% (80 students) have a documented prior mental health history; within High Stress, 71.4% (35 of 49) have prior history.
  • 60.0% (120 students) report no prior history, yet this group includes 66 Medium-stress and 14 High-stress students, highlighting substantial first-onset university distress.
  • Only 9.0% (18 students) use psychiatric medication, with 15 of 18 belonging to the High Stress tier.
Transition: For students engaged in active psychological support, which therapy modalities demonstrate empirical recovery gains?

Scene 5 — Intervention Patterns and Recovery Progress

Core Question: Which therapeutic interventions demonstrate measurable symptom recovery?

  • Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) leads recovery with an average progress score of 40.80 across 7.50 weeks.
  • Counseling (34.43), Support Groups (33.10), and Meditation (32.57) all provide measurable symptom relief.
  • Institutional Care Gap: 107 of 200 students (53.5%) receive "No Therapy", including 15 High-stress and 48 Medium-stress students.
Policy Recommendations: Scale accessible evidence-based CBT programs, embed campus mindfulness and sleep hygiene workshops, and implement proactive first-year mental health screening.
