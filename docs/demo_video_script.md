## 🎙️ Verbatim Narration Script

### Section 1: Introduction & Problem Statement (00:00 – 00:35)
> *"Hello everyone. My name is Sufiyan Surve, and this project is titled Analysing Mental Health in Student Ecosystem. Completed as part of the Data Analytics with Tableau Virtual Internship on SkillWallet by SmartBridge, this project delivers an empirical diagnostic framework for university student wellness. In this video walkthrough, I will take you through our live public project showcase, demonstrating our end-to-end analytics workflow from problem formulation and data preparation to interactive dashboards, narrative data storytelling, and empirical clinical insights."*

---

### Section 2: Cohort Overview & KPI Indicators (00:35 – 01:15)
> *"Looking at the cohort overview and key performance indicators: our study encompasses exactly 200 student records across 18 validated variables. The baseline metrics reveal an Average Anxiety Score of 52.59 out of 100, an Average Depression Score of 48.09 out of 100, and an Average Daily Screen Time of 7.10 hours. Notice that in the high-stress student segment, anxiety surges to an alarming 72.06, while depression rises to 68.78. These baseline indicators establish the serious emotional burden carried by university students."*

---

### Section 3: Methodology & Data Integrity (01:15 – 01:51)
> *"Moving to the Methodology and Data Integrity section: the project follows a disciplined six-stage pipeline: dataset validation, rigorous data hygiene, visual analytics, responsive dashboard design, guided data storytelling, and web showcase integration. We verified all 200 unique student IDs with zero null values across all 3,600 data points. Continuous measures obey strict clinical boundaries, while categorical attributes are systematically normalized. This solid foundation ensures reliable, bias-free institutional reporting."*

---

### Section 4: Executive Diagnostic Dashboard (01:51 – 02:33)
> *"Now, let us examine the Executive Diagnostic Dashboard. This multi-device command center brings together four top-level KPI indicators with six visual diagnostic panels. Notice the central distribution: 78.0 percent of all students experience elevated distress, with 53.5 percent in Medium stress and 24.5 percent in High stress. Only 22 percent remain in the Low-stress category. Furthermore, 71.4 percent of high-stress students suffer from poor sleep, and their daily screen immersion climbs to 8.12 hours compared to 6.00 hours for low-stress peers."*

---

### Section 5: Story Scene 1 — Student Mental Health Baseline (02:33 – 03:03)
> *"Next, we navigate through our guided 5-Scene Data Story. Scene 1 establishes the Cohort Baseline. Here we see the macro distribution: out of 200 students, 44 are classified as Low stress, 107 as Medium stress, and 49 as High stress. More than three out of every four students operate under chronic emotional pressure, proving that student mental health is a systemic university challenge requiring proactive institutional support."*

---

### Section 6: Story Scene 2 — Stress & Psychological Symptoms (03:03 – 03:42)
> *"Scene 2 examines Stress and Psychological Symptoms. As we inspect symptom escalation, subjective perceived stress couples directly with standardized clinical loads. Anxiety scores leap from 30.27 in low-stress students to 72.06 in high-stress students, an increase of over 138 percent. Depression follows the exact same steep trajectory, rising by 156 percent from 26.80 to 68.78. Both male and female cohorts reflect comparable symptom burdens, showing universal emotional vulnerability across genders."*

---

### Section 7: Story Scene 3 — Lifestyle Factors & Stress Compounding (03:42 – 04:16)
> *"Scene 3 focuses on Lifestyle Factors and Stress Compounding. This reveals the critical behavioral triad: sleep deprivation, digital screen immersion, and physical inactivity. Approximately 71.4 percent of high-stress students report poor sleep quality, compared to only 15.9 percent in the low-stress group. Daily screen time averages 8.12 hours under high stress, and over 75 percent engage in minimal exercise, demonstrating how modern digital habits amplify emotional exhaustion."*

---

### Section 8: Story Scene 4 — Vulnerability Factors & Support Systems (04:16 – 04:46)
> *"Scene 4 investigates Vulnerability Factors and Support Systems. 40 percent of students entered university with a pre-existing clinical history, yet 60 percent had no prior diagnosis, meaning 14 high-stress cases emerged as first-onset university distress. Crucially, the data proves that students with strong peer support networks maintain substantially lower anxiety levels, highlighting the vital role of university social circles as a protective buffer."*

---

### Section 9: Story Scene 5 — Intervention Patterns & Clinical Recovery (04:46 – 05:19)
> *"Scene 5 analyzes Intervention Patterns and Clinical Recovery. Among active intervention modalities, structured Cognitive Behavioral Therapy achieves the highest empirical progress score at 40.80 out of 100, followed by counseling, support groups, and mindfulness meditation. However, the data exposes an urgent institutional care gap: 53.5 percent of the student body receives no therapy whatsoever, leaving 15 high-stress and 48 medium-stress individuals without clinical guidance."*

---

### Section 10: Key Empirical Discoveries (05:19 – 05:51)
> *"Reviewing our Key Empirical Discoveries: four central takeaways emerge. First, pervasive stress affects 78 percent of students. Second, severe symptom escalation sees anxiety and depression surge by over 135 percent. Third, behavioral compounding links poor sleep and over 8 hours of screen immersion directly to elevated strain. And fourth, a substantial institutional care gap leaves more than half the student body without formal psychological assistance."*

---

### Section 11: Institutional Recommendations & Conclusion (05:51 – 06:24)
> *"In conclusion, this project demonstrates how rigorous data preparation, diagnostic dashboard design, and guided visual storytelling transform raw student surveys into actionable institutional intelligence. We recommend expanding accessible CBT counseling on campus, introducing digital wellness workshops, and implementing early-semester wellness screenings. Please note that native Tableau Public workbook upload is pending manual authoring, while our complete analytical solution is fully accessible here on GitHub Pages. Thank you for your time."*
